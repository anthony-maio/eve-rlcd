# Results: RLCD on Qwen3-0.6B-Base

Generated by `scripts/consolidate_results.py` from the run directories under `runs/`; every number is computed from `preds.jsonl`, `probe.json`, `meta.json` and `train_log.jsonl` with `rlcd.eval` and `rlcd.metrics`. Do not edit by hand. Lower is better for ECE, Brier loss and NOTA false alarm; higher is better for accuracy and NOTA recall. Cells with three values are mean [min, max] across the runs of that arm.

## Setup

Base model `Qwen/Qwen3-0.6B-Base` (full fine-tune, fp32 master weights, bf16 autocast, AdamW with betas 0.9 and 0.95 and weight decay 0.1, cosine schedule to a floor of 0.1 x peak after a linear warmup of 5 percent of the steps). Data: `data/train.jsonl` has 64,000 rows, `data/val.jsonl` 8,000, `data/test.jsonl` 8,000; every number below is on the full 8,000-row test split at temperature 1 with prompts truncated to 512 tokens. Warmup: supervised, rows 0..6399 (6,400 rows), 1 epoch, 64 prompts per step, 100 steps, peak lr 2e-05. RL arms: rows 32000..63999 (32,000 rows, disjoint from the warmup rows), 2 epochs, 128 prompts per step with 4 sampled actions per prompt (group size 4, leave-one-out baseline), 500 steps, no KL term (kl 0.0), peak lr 2e-05 for RLCD, the oracle and the shared-settings RLVR arm, and 4e-06 for the RLVR low-lr arm. RLCD's reward is the outcome minus the stated probability of the taken action, RLVR's the outcome alone; the oracle sees the label and minimizes its NLL on the same rows. Stop rule, checked at every evaluation (every 50 steps on a 2,000-row stride sample of the validation split): "eval_acc more than 0.10 below step 0 for three consecutive evals"; it fired only for the shared-settings RLVR run. Supervised continuation control: the Colab warmup trained for 5 more epochs on the same 6,400 rows, 64 prompts per step, 500 steps, peak lr 2e-05. 32k-label SFT reference: rows 0..31999 from the base model, 1 epoch, 64 prompts per step, 500 steps. Hardware: the local runs on one RTX 4080 (micro-batch 4 x 16 accumulation for SFT, 4 x 32 for RL, gradient checkpointing on); the Colab runs on an A100 (micro-batch 8 x 8 for SFT, 8 x 16 for RL, gradient checkpointing off; the run files do not record the GPU name, the notebook asks for an A100 runtime), with the same effective batch. The Colab seed runs started from the Colab warmup (seed 0, trained on the A100), not from the local warmup.

## What the numbers show

1. RLCD learns from bandit feedback and keeps the warmup's calibration. Across the three seeds RLCD's test accuracy is 0.808 [0.806, 0.811] against 0.748 for the warmups it started from, with ECE 0.023 [0.020, 0.029] (warmups 0.022) and mean confidence 0.830 against accuracy 0.808. Paired against its own warmup, RLCD's accuracy difference is seed 0 +0.062 [+0.053, +0.072], seed 1 +0.056 [+0.047, +0.066], seed 2 +0.061 [+0.052, +0.070] (all three intervals exclude zero); Brier seed 0 -0.071 [-0.079, -0.061], seed 1 -0.071 [-0.079, -0.062], seed 2 -0.075 [-0.084, -0.067] (all three intervals exclude zero); ECE seed 0 -0.005 [-0.014, +0.003], seed 1 +0.001 [-0.009, +0.009], seed 2 +0.010 [-0.002, +0.018] (none of the three intervals excludes zero). The accuracy and Brier gains are resolved on every seed. The ECE change is at most +0.010 and no seed's interval excludes zero, though seed 2's interval only just includes it. 500 RL steps left calibration within about 0.01 ECE of where the warmup was; RLCD did not improve it.

2. RLVR with the outcome-only reward does not keep it. At the shared learning rate it collapses inside 50 steps and the stop rule ends it at step 150: test accuracy 0.576, mean confidence 0.991, ECE 0.415, Brier 0.835 (RLCD minus this run: accuracy +0.232 [+0.220, +0.244], ECE -0.394 [-0.405, -0.380]). At lr 4e-6 it keeps its accuracy (0.778 mean over three seeds, above the warmups) but its mean confidence is 0.991 [0.989, 0.993], ECE 0.213 and Brier 0.432, a worse Brier than the warmup it started from (0.339). RLCD minus RLVR low lr, per seed: accuracy seed 0 +0.032 [+0.024, +0.040], seed 1 +0.026 [+0.018, +0.034], seed 2 +0.032 [+0.024, +0.041] (all three intervals exclude zero); ECE seed 0 -0.195 [-0.202, -0.184], seed 1 -0.189 [-0.197, -0.179], seed 2 -0.185 [-0.194, -0.176]; Brier seed 0 -0.171 [-0.184, -0.156], seed 1 -0.155 [-0.168, -0.142], seed 2 -0.170 [-0.184, -0.156]. The two arms differ in the reward (RLCD subtracts p(taken action) from the outcome) and in the learning rate (RLVR low lr uses 4e-06 because at 2e-05 it collapses); RLCD was not run at the lower rate.

3. Against the oracle, which sees the label on the same rows, RLCD gives up about one accuracy point and is better calibrated. RLCD minus oracle, per seed: accuracy seed 0 -0.014 [-0.021, -0.007], seed 1 -0.011 [-0.018, -0.005], seed 2 -0.008 [-0.014, -0.001] (all three intervals exclude zero); ECE seed 0 -0.025 [-0.031, -0.017], seed 1 -0.039 [-0.045, -0.031], seed 2 -0.043 [-0.049, -0.035] (all three intervals exclude zero); Brier seed 0 +0.016 [+0.009, +0.022], seed 1 +0.013 [+0.007, +0.020], seed 2 +0.006 [-0.001, +0.012] (two of the three intervals exclude zero). The oracle's mean confidence is 0.878 against accuracy 0.819: two epochs of supervised training on the same rows make it overconfident, and bandit feedback with the RLCD reward does not.

4. The gain is not just more steps. The supervised continuation trained the Colab warmup for 500 more steps on its own 6,400 labeled rows and reached accuracy 0.778 with ECE 0.192 and mean confidence 0.969: it gains accuracy and loses calibration the way the low-lr RLVR arm does. RLCD minus the continuation, seeds 1 and 2: accuracy +0.028 [+0.020, +0.037] and +0.033 [+0.025, +0.042]; ECE -0.172 [-0.180, -0.161] and -0.163 [-0.172, -0.154]; Brier -0.138 [-0.150, -0.124] and -0.142 [-0.156, -0.129]. 32,000 bandit rows with the RLCD reward are worth more than five more passes over the 6,400 labels, on accuracy and on calibration.

5. Against the 32k-label SFT reference (500 supervised steps on 32,000 labeled rows, one local run), RLCD with 6,400 labels plus 32,000 bandit rows is close but behind: accuracy -0.009 [-0.017, -0.002], ECE +0.007 [-0.002, +0.014], Brier +0.017 [+0.011, +0.024]. The oracle, which had the labels of the same 32,000 rows plus the warmup's 6,400, matches the reference on accuracy (+0.005 [-0.003, +0.012]) and Brier (+0.002 [-0.005, +0.009]) and is worse calibrated (+0.032 [+0.022, +0.038]). The reference's ECE (0.014) is the lowest of any run here: one pass over 32,000 labels kept its calibration where two passes over the same labels (the oracle) and five passes over 6,400 (the continuation) lost it.

6. On the known-posterior probe RLCD's confidence moves toward the posterior on the ambiguous tickets. On tickets with one cued department (posterior 1.0) RLCD's mean max p is 0.973 (warmups 0.984); on tickets with two cued departments (posterior 0.5 each) it is 0.593, down from 0.798 for the warmups. The oracle (0.630) and the SFT reference (0.598) get there with the labels; RLVR low lr says 0.990 on the same ambiguous tickets, and the supervised continuation moves the wrong way (0.915). One difference from the labeled arms: RLCD keeps 0.915 of the mass on the two cued departments against 0.998 for the oracle and 0.982 for the warmups, so part of its lower max p is mass that leaked to the uncued departments rather than a fairer split between the cued two. On the escalate question (posterior 0.90) RLCD's mean confidence is 0.932, the oracle's 0.880, the warmups' 0.821, the SFT reference's 0.935 and RLVR low lr's 1.000.

## Caveats

- One warmup per hardware. The local RL arms all started from the local warmup (seed 0, RTX 4080); the Colab seed 1 and seed 2 arms both started from the one Colab warmup (seed 0, A100). The warmup row of the main table therefore averages two runs, not three, and the two Colab seeds share their starting point.
- "Seed" varies more than the sampling. Seeds 1 and 2 differ from seed 0 in the RL sampling seed, in the warmup checkpoint, in the GPU, in the micro-batch shape and in gradient checkpointing; the effective batch and every other setting are the same. The seed spread in the tables therefore includes hardware and warmup variation, and a difference between seed 0 and seeds 1 and 2 is not a pure seed effect.
- bf16 is not deterministic. Training and the evaluations ran under bf16 autocast, and a bf16 kernel's rounding changes with the batch shape. Scoring runs/q-rlcd in fp32 through the decision API (runs/decide-bench/preds_ask.jsonl) instead of the bf16 evaluation moves accuracy by 0.0006, ECE by 0.0004 and Brier by under 0.0001 on the same 8000 rows. Two local shared-settings RLVR attempts with the same seed and command took different trajectories (validation accuracy 0.617 and 0.534 at step 50, from runs/q-rlvr and runs/q-rlvr-b), so training is not bit-reproducible either. The third decimal is printed so the tables agree with the files; it is not stable.
- The shared-settings RLVR arm has one run, stopped at step 150 by the rule "eval_acc more than 0.10 below step 0 for three consecutive evals", and its numbers are those of the stopped checkpoint; it was not rerun on Colab. The three-seed RLVR comparison is the low-lr arm.
- ECE. Plug-in ECE is biased upward and its percentile bootstrap interval inherits the bias (the largest bootstrap-mean-minus-point-estimate across these runs is 0.003), so the per-run intervals of well calibrated runs sit above the point estimate and overlap of two runs' intervals says little. The paired differences resample the same rows for both runs, so the row-to-row agreement cancels; those are the intervals to read. They cover test-row sampling only, not training noise: a paired interval that excludes zero on one seed says that one pair of checkpoints differs on this split, and the three seeds are the only estimate of training noise here.
- The supervised continuation is one run on one warmup at a batch of 64 prompts per step (the warmup's batch), not the RL arms' 128; it answers "does 500 more supervised steps on the same 6,400 labels give what RLCD gives", not "does any supervised schedule". The 32k-label SFT reference trained on rows 0..31999 from the base model, while the RL arms saw 6,400 of those rows as labels plus rows 32000..63999 as bandit rows, so that comparison is by row count, not identical rows.
- Everything is in-distribution: the test split is a held-out slice of the same public classification datasets and the same synthetic triage generator the model trained on, 8,000 rows, prompts of at most 512 tokens. The known-posterior probe is fresh synthetic triage tickets whose true posterior the generator fixes; it says nothing about calibration on real tickets or on longer prompts. No out-of-distribution evaluation was run.
- RLCD's calibration during training is not flat. Validation ECE over the 500 RL steps (see curves.png): local/rlcd peaks at 0.064 at step 400 and ends at 0.017; colab/rlcd-s1 peaks at 0.067 at step 50 and ends at 0.030; colab/rlcd-s2 peaks at 0.123 at step 50 and ends at 0.032. The final checkpoint is what is reported; no checkpoint selection was done, but a reader should not take the final ECE as a guarantee at every step. On the escalate probe question RLCD's mean confidence is 0.913, 0.939, 0.944 across the seeds against a posterior of 0.90, so it sits a little above the posterior there.
- The stopped RLVR arm and the low-lr arm are the same reward at two learning rates, and the low-lr arm is compared with RLCD at 2e-05, five times its rate; RLCD at 4e-06 was not run. No other RLVR regularization (a KL term, an entropy bonus, a temperature) was tried on this base, so "RLVR miscalibrates" means "this REINFORCE-with-outcome-reward recipe miscalibrates at these settings", not that no outcome-reward recipe could be made to work.
